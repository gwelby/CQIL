Bell Pair Entanglement

The Bell pair is the simplest form of quantum entanglement, involving just two quantum particles. When these particles are entangled, their quantum states become correlated in such a way that the state of one particle instantaneously determines the state of the other, regardless of the distance between them.

This "spooky action at a distance" (as Einstein called it) demonstrates the non-local nature of quantum mechanics. A measurement on one particle immediately affects its entangled partner, even if separated by vast distances, creating a connection that appears to transmit information faster than light.

Bell Pair Entanglement

🔗

The foundational two-particle entanglement where measurement of one particle instantaneously determines the state of the other. Bell inequality violations confirm the non-local nature of quantum reality.

  • Two maximally entangled qubits
  • Forms basis for quantum teleportation
  • Used in quantum key distribution

GHZ State Entanglement

⚡

Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger states extend Bell pairs to three or more particles with maximal correlation. All particles share a global quantum state with perfect correlation in measurement outcomes.

  • N-particle maximal correlation
  • All-or-nothing quantum effects
  • Strongest demonstration of non-locality

W State Entanglement

🌊

W states represent distributed excitation across multiple particles. Unlike GHZ states, W states maintain entanglement even when particles are lost, showing remarkable robustness to decoherence.

  • Distributes single excitation
  • Robust to particle loss
  • Maintains entanglement under measurement

Cluster State Entanglement

🕸️

Graph-based entanglement with particles connected in lattice structures. These highly entangled states form the foundation of measurement-based quantum computing and quantum error correction.

  • Graph-based quantum connections
  • Foundation for quantum computing
  • Enables one-way quantum computation
